\chapter{Capability Matrix}\label{app:capability-matrix}

\Cref{sec:be-matrix} introduced the capability matrix and reproduced its
transport, notification, asynchrony, and buffering columns for all ten backends
(\cref{tab:capabilities}). This appendix gives the matrix from the complementary
direction --- the one a schedule author needs --- by recording, for each backend,
the concurrency substrate it maps a worker onto, and then tabulating which schedule
\emph{demands} each backend accepts. Together these answer the practical question
the matrix exists to answer: given a schedule, which backends can compile it, and
which reject it and why.

\section{Backends and their concurrency substrate}

The capability columns of \cref{tab:capabilities} say what IO mechanisms a backend
offers; \cref{tab:cap-substrate} says what a worker physically becomes on each
backend and over what substrate workers communicate. The two together are the full
declaration each backend commits as reviewable text.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
    \toprule
    Backend & Tier & Worker maps to & Substrate \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{openmp-rs}       & 1 & task on a work-stealing pool & shared memory \\
    \texttt{pthreads-sync}   & 1 & operating-system thread      & shared memory \\
    \texttt{pthreads-async}  & 1 & operating-system thread      & shared memory, ring buffers \\
    \texttt{mp-tcp-bufsync}  & 1 & operating-system process     & TCP loopback socket \\
    \texttt{mp-tcp-poll}     & 1 & operating-system process     & TCP loopback socket \\
    \texttt{mp-tcp-event}    & 1 & operating-system process     & TCP loopback socket \\
    \texttt{mp-uds-event}    & 1 & operating-system process     & Unix-domain socket \\
    \texttt{mpi-blocking}    & 2 & MPI rank                     & MPI \\
    \texttt{mpi-nonblocking} & 2 & MPI rank                     & MPI \\
    \texttt{embedded-pattern}& 3 & microcontroller core         & UART fabric, DMA/PIO \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}

  how workers communicate. The two shared-memory synchronous backends,
  \texttt{openmp-rs} and \texttt{pthreads-sync}, are deliberately

  raw threads), so a disagreement between just those two would implicate the
  substrate rather than the schedule.}
  \label{tab:cap-substrate}
\end{table}

\section{Schedule demands and backend acceptance}

Orthogonality means backend choice is mediated only by the matrix: a schedule
compiles against a backend exactly when the backend can satisfy what the schedule
demands, and is rejected at compile time, with a message naming the missing
capability, otherwise (\cref{sec:be-matrix}). The demands fall into two kinds, and
the resolution rule differs between them. The \texttt{async} and \texttt{buffer}
demands are \emph{ordered}: asking for asynchrony, or for a buffer depth $N$,
succeeds against any backend whose declared maximum meets or exceeds it, so this
axis is a genuine superset relation. The notification discipline is instead a
\emph{partition}: a schedule's \texttt{notify} selects exactly one discipline, and a
backend accepts it precisely when that discipline is in the backend's declared
notify-set, rejecting the others. The disciplines do not form a lattice ---
\texttt{notify=poll} is not ``less than'' \texttt{notify=event} --- so a backend
offering only the event discipline rejects a poll-notified schedule and vice versa.
\Cref{tab:cap-demands} tabulates both kinds for the four IO demands a schedule can
raise. A schedule that raises none of them --- a single-worker schedule, or a
multi-worker schedule using only synchronous, unbuffered, blocking transfers ---
compiles on every backend.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
    \toprule
    Backend & \texttt{async} & \texttt{buffer>1} & \texttt{notify=event} & \texttt{notify=poll} \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{openmp-rs}       & --- & --- & --- & --- \\
    \texttt{pthreads-sync}   & --- & --- & --- & --- \\
    \texttt{pthreads-async}  & yes & yes (to 64) & yes & --- \\
    \texttt{mp-tcp-bufsync}  & --- & --- & --- & --- \\
    \texttt{mp-tcp-poll}     & --- & --- & --- & yes \\
    \texttt{mp-tcp-event}    & yes & yes (to 64) & yes & --- \\
    \texttt{mp-uds-event}    & yes & yes (to 64) & yes & --- \\
    \texttt{mpi-blocking}    & --- & --- & --- & --- \\
    \texttt{mpi-nonblocking} & yes & yes (to 64) & yes & --- \\
    \texttt{embedded-pattern}& --- & --- & --- & --- \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Which schedule IO demand each backend accepts. ``\texttt{async}'' is an
  asynchronous transfer; ``\texttt{buffer>1}'' is an in-flight depth greater than
  one, up to the stated maximum; the two \texttt{notify} columns are the wakeup
  disciplines. A dash means the backend rejects a schedule raising that demand, at
  compile time. Every backend additionally accepts the synchronous, unbuffered,
  barrier-or-blocking baseline, which is why a schedule using only that baseline is
  universally portable. \texttt{mpi-nonblocking} is a strict superset of
  \texttt{mpi-blocking}.}
  \label{tab:cap-demands}
\end{table}

Two consequences of this table are worth drawing out, as they govern how the
differential matrix is populated. First, a schedule that demands an asynchronous,
event-notified, double-buffered transfer is accepted only by the four asynchronous
backends (\texttt{pthreads-async}, \texttt{mp-tcp-event}, \texttt{mp-uds-event},
and \texttt{mpi-nonblocking}); the matrix records such a schedule's \emph{required}
column --- the set of backends the harness must hold to byte-identity --- as exactly
those four, and the synchronous backends correctly reject it rather than silently
downgrading. Second, the polled discipline is offered by only one
backend, \texttt{mp-tcp-poll}, so a schedule that specifically demands
\texttt{notify=poll} isolates that backend's notification path. The matrix is what
makes these required-column choices mechanical rather than ad hoc: the set of
backends a cell requires is computed from the schedule's demands against these
declarations, and the harness then holds exactly that set to byte-identity.
