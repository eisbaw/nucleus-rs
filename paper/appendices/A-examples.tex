\chapter{Example Catalogue}\label{app:examples}

The corpus of worked examples is the system's specification (\cref{sec:val-suite}).
This appendix catalogues the twenty-eight examples and, for each, names what it
computes and the scheduling or compiler property it is there to stress. Most examples carry more than one schedule --- typically a single-worker
reference schedule and one or more distributed or transformed schedules that must
reproduce the reference output byte-for-byte --- so the number of differential cells
is several times the number of examples.

The examples are grouped by the concern they principally exercise, in six tables
(\cref{tab:examples-foundational,tab:examples-stencil,tab:examples-reduction,%
tab:examples-indexing,tab:examples-pipelines,tab:examples-boundary}). The grouping is
expository only; many examples touch more than one concern, and the table notes the
primary one.

\section{Reductions and the combine algebra}

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.20\textwidth}p{0.34\textwidth}p{0.38\textwidth}@{}}
    \toprule
    Example & Computes & Stresses \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{03-reduction} & A sum reduction to a scalar & The distributed
      accumulator pattern: per-worker partials combined at a host under an
      associative, commutative operator. \\
    \texttt{25-bin-parity} & Per-bin parity (count modulo two) & A bitwise-xor
      combine with a zero identity; an operator-form rather than method-form
      accumulator. \\
    \texttt{26-bin-min} & Per-bin minimum over keyed values & A minimum combine
      whose identity is the type maximum, not zero; identity-aware accumulator
      initialisation, with an empty bin forcing the identity to show. \\
    \texttt{27-bin-fmin} & Per-bin floating-point minimum & An order-independent
      \emph{float} combine that is admitted directly (with its $+\infty$ identity),
      in contrast to a plain float \emph{sum}, which is rejected for
      non-associativity. \\
    \texttt{28-bin-fsum} & Per-bin reproducible floating-point sum & The opt-in
      \texttt{combine=fsum}: a distributed float \emph{sum} made cross-backend
      bit-identical by a fixed worker-id-sorted fold (the reference oracle moving in
      lockstep), as distinct from plain \texttt{sum} (still rejected) and from a
      schedule-invariant order-independent reduction (future work). \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Reduction examples spanning the supported combine operators.}
  \label{tab:examples-reduction}
\end{table}

\section{Embedded and boundary cases}

\begin{table}[htbp]

  \small

    \toprule
    Example & Computes & Stresses \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{14-hearing-aid} & A three-core hearing-aid signal pipeline & The

    \texttt{22-dma-pio-demo} & A fixed-point gain stage & Per-edge transport-mode

    \texttt{04-prefix-sum} & An inclusive prefix sum & A carried recurrence expressed
      as a multi-pass rectangular accumulator, since the surface forbids a carried
      scan directly. \\
    \texttt{21-jacobi-converge} & A Jacobi sweep with convergence-driven termination &
      The deliberate boundary marker: a data-dependent loop exit that compiles and
      runs only on the single-worker path and is recorded as a skip elsewhere. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Embedded examples and the boundary cases that mark the edges of the
  supported class.}
  \label{tab:examples-boundary}
\end{table}

The last two rows of \cref{tab:examples-boundary} are the most informative for a
reader probing the limits: \texttt{04-prefix-sum} shows how a pattern outside the
surface grammar (a carried scan) is expressed within it as a kernel-level idiom, and
\texttt{21-jacobi-converge} shows where the static-firing-order boundary is drawn and
how the corpus records, rather than hides, a shape it does not yet support across all
paths.
