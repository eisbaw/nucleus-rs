## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
Turn the internal knowledge the compiler already has into a teaching tool. Today --emit-pn dumps a Graphviz net; everything else (inferred halos, synthesised Push/Wait pairs, per-worker partition bands, reuse windows, why the net is sound) lives only in the sidecars and is invisible. Add a subcommand or flag that explains, in prose, for a given (algo, sched, backend): why each transfer was synthesised and whether it is whole-array or a contiguous span and why; the halo width inferred per partitioned axis; each worker's band; what pipeline=D and reuse changed; and, on a rejection, the narrated story (worker w2 waits for img_in seq 4 but the host push is behind the barrier you placed at ...). This is a genuinely novel DX feature no competitor has, and it doubles as the answer to why is my schedule deadlocking. Read-only over the existing pipeline output; emits no code.
<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:END -->
